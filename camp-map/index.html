How the rating works

It's a rule of thumb built from the official NWS forecast for the next 3 days (sooner days count a little more). A spot rates best with highs from about 60 to 82°F, nights above about 40°F, low rain chances, wind under 15 mph, and no thunderstorms, snow or ice. Active NWS warnings pull a spot's rating down a lot, and watches and advisories a little. Spots are straight-line distances, so the drive will be longer. Over water or outside the U.S. there's no NWS forecast, so it tries a nearby spot on land instead.

More places to stay

Federal public land is often the cheapest place to camp. The private hosts are paid marketplaces, not public land; they fill the gap when free camping isn't close.

  • Recreation.govPublic land. The official site for federal campgrounds, cabins and permits from the Park Service, Forest Service, BLM, Army Corps and more.
  • BLM public landsPublic land. Free dispersed camping on Bureau of Land Management land, mostly in the West. Stays are generally limited to 14 days in any 28-day period; rules vary by area.
  • Harvest HostsOvernight parking at wineries, farms and breweries. Membership required.
  • Boondockers WelcomeDriveway camping hosted by fellow RVers and van-lifers.
  • HipcampPrivate land, farms and backyard camping spots you book by the night.
  • SniffspotRent a private, fenced yard by the hour so the dogs can run on a travel day.
  • FreeCampsites.netA crowdsourced list of free camping spots: a good second opinion next to this map.
