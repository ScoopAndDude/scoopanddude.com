Places the past refuses to leave

Tower of London

London, England

Nearly a millennium of imprisonment, torture, and execution. Anne Boleyn’s headless ghost is the most frequently reported; the Princes in the Tower and countless others are also said to remain.

Reported phenomena: Headless female figure in Tudor dress, silent processions, cold spots, footsteps in locked corridors.

History note: Anne Boleyn was executed on Tower Green in 1536. Multiple independent accounts from Yeoman Warders and visitors span the 19th and 20th centuries.

Visitor tip: Standard admission includes the site of her execution and the Chapel of St Peter ad Vincula. Evening tours sometimes emphasize the ghost stories.

The Stanley Hotel

Estes Park, Colorado, USA

Inspiration for Stephen King’s The Shining. Room 217 (site of a 1911 explosion) and the ghosts of founders F.O. and Flora Stanley are among the most reported.

Reported phenomena: Piano music with no pianist, children’s laughter, lights switching, Flora Stanley’s presence in the music room.

History note: In September 1974 King stayed in Room 217, dreamed of a fire hose chasing his son, and outlined the novel by morning.

Visitor tip: Fully operating hotel. Ghost tours and overnight investigations available; Room 217 can be requested.

Paris Catacombs

Paris, France

The remains of more than six million people, transferred from overflowing cemeteries and arranged in elaborate patterns beneath the city.

Reported phenomena: Sudden temperature drops, whispered voices, overwhelming sense of presence.

History note: Begun in the late 18th century to solve the city’s cemetery crisis. The public section is only a small fraction of the network.

Visitor tip: Timed tickets required. The official tour is well-lit and guided; unauthorized sections are illegal and dangerous.

Edinburgh Castle

Edinburgh, Scotland

Volcanic rock fortress with dungeons that held soldiers and civilians. Ghosts of prisoners, a spectral piper, and a dog in the cemetery are commonly reported.

Reported phenomena: Phantom piper, colonial and French soldiers, dog in the pet cemetery, cold spots in the dungeons.

Visitor tip: Open year-round. The castle’s own ghost tours or evening events are the best way to hear the stories on-site.

Poveglia Island

Venice Lagoon, Italy

Plague quarantine station where tens of thousands died, later an asylum. Locals largely avoid it; stories of a malevolent doctor persist.

Reported phenomena: Feelings of dread, apparitions, unexplained illness among those who stay overnight.

Visitor tip: Not open to the public. Boat tours may circle the island; landing is prohibited without permission.

Asia Leave before sunset

Bhangarh Fort

Rajasthan, India

India’s most famous haunted fort. A wizard’s curse is said to prevent anyone from living within its walls after dark. Official signs enforce the rule.

Reported phenomena: Voices, footsteps, and an oppressive atmosphere after dusk.

Visitor tip: Archaeological Survey of India site. Open during daylight; entry after sunset is forbidden and enforced.

Island of the Dolls

Xochimilco, Mexico

Hundreds of decaying dolls hang from trees, placed by a caretaker who believed they were possessed by the spirit of a drowned girl.

Visitor tip: Reachable by trajinera (traditional boat) from Xochimilco. Daytime visits only; the visual impact is strong.

Winchester Mystery House

San Jose, California, USA

Sarah Winchester continuously expanded the house for 38 years—doors to nowhere, staircases to ceilings—allegedly to confuse the spirits of those killed by Winchester rifles.

Reported phenomena: Footsteps, cold spots, apparitions of Sarah herself, doors that open and close.

Visitor tip: Guided mansion tours available year-round; special Halloween events in fall.

DEEPER ACCOUNTS

Stories that refuse to fade

Anne Boleyn’s Procession

In 1864 a Yeoman Warder confronted a white female figure near the execution site; his bayonet passed through her. Earlier accounts describe a blue-white light in the locked Chapel of St Peter ad Vincula and a silent Tudor procession led by the headless queen. She is also reported at Hever Castle and Hampton Court Palace.

The Nightmare That Became The Shining

In late September 1974 Stephen King and his wife were the only guests at the Stanley Hotel as it closed for winter. That night he dreamed his young son was chased down the corridors by a living fire hose. By morning the novel’s outline was complete. They stayed in Room 217—the same room where a 1911 gas explosion injured chambermaid Elizabeth Wilson. Her ghost is still said to tidy the room.

The Curse of Bhangarh

Legend holds that a tantrik, rejected by the princess, cursed the fort so that it would never again be inhabited. Whether or not the curse is literal, the site has remained empty and official signs still require visitors to leave before sunset.

These accounts blend verified history with long-running visitor and staff reports. Belief is optional; the atmosphere is not. Always check current access rules, respect private property, and leave before dark where required.
